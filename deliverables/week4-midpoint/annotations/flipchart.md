## The source

- **What it is.** Two photographs of a flip chart written during the group's own
  structured brainstorm, `flipchart-1-opportunity-and-evidence.jpeg` and
  `flipchart-2-proposed-tool-and-questions.jpeg`, plus
  `structured-brainstorm-notes.md`, a typed version of the same session.
- **Who made it.** The group, unaided. This is the only Week 3 source with no
  tool between the group and the page, which is why it is worth annotating
  separately from the reports written about it.
- **When.** `GAPS.md` in this folder dates the sheets and the typed notes to
  11 September 2026. The files themselves carry no date that could be verified
  here, and the MapIt manifest separately lists a photograph taken on
  16 September, so the two should not be assumed to be the same photograph.
- **How it was read.** The three files are in this working tree under
  `evidence/raw/week3/flipchart/` and were read in place: the photographs with
  an image reading tool, the notes as text.
- **A caution about the quotations below.** The sheets are handwritten, and
  every phrase quoted from a photograph is **a transcription made by the same
  agent that wrote the annotation**. Any of them could be misread. A group
  member should check each quoted phrase against the sheet before the summary
  repeats it.
- No person is named on either sheet and none is named here.

## Annotations

### C1. During the meeting is the whole differentiator

> "Similair Programs are post-meeting" (sheet 1, top right, spelling as written)

**Labels:** useful / keep
**Why:** It is the sharpest line on either sheet and the one claim the rest of
the project is built on, so it belongs in the proposal in the group's own hand
rather than paraphrased.

### C2. An unchecked claim about the competition

> "CEO have this already" (sheet 1, top right)

**Labels:** surprising / question
**Why:** The sheet asserts that such tools already exist for some users without
naming one or saying who checked, so it should be verified before a slide claims
the during-meeting space is open, and it is the only competitive claim in the
whole Week 3 material.

### C3. Visual conclusions, not more text

> "Not just text, but visual conclusions" (sheet 1, section 2, Evidence)

**Labels:** useful / keep
**Why:** This is the group's own statement of what a text summary fails to do,
and it is the reason the product outputs a canvas rather than minutes.

### C4. The evidence section cites a tool but records nothing about it

> "Evidence -> Miro AI..." (sheet 1, section 2)

**Labels:** unconvincing / question
**Why:** A competing tool is named under the heading Evidence with nothing
written about what was tried, what it did or why it fell short, so the section
labelled evidence currently carries none, and either somebody tried it and the
note is missing or nobody did.

### C5. The core is named, and it is the riskiest part

> "real life accurate transcriptions, core commands recognition" (sheet 2, section 5)

**Labels:** useful / keep
**Why:** The group identified speech and command recognition as the core on the
day, which matches the repository's own statement that the speech path is the
riskiest assumption and is not built, so the proposal can say the group knew
this before the tooling told it.

### C6. The group knew the scope was too wide

> "Too wide? Needs to be narrowed down?" (sheet 2, section 5)

**Labels:** useful / keep
**Why:** The self-criticism is on the sheet in the group's own hand, which makes
the later narrowing to one meeting and one wall a decision the group reached
rather than a constraint imposed by the build.

### C7. An architecture chosen before a capability was tested

> "Multiple AI agents?" (sheet 2, extra ideas, and repeated in the typed notes)

**Labels:** unconvincing / reject
**Why:** Splitting the work across a transcribing agent, a command agent and a
visuals agent is a structure picked before anything had been measured, it buys
nothing the group named a problem for, and nothing in the rest of the material
depends on it.

### C8. Platform undecided, with a lean

> "App? Website?" (sheet 2, section 5, with Website underlined)

**Labels:** unclear / keep
**Why:** The underline reads as a lean towards the browser rather than a
decision, and the built client is a browser client, so the proposal can note
that the direction was chosen on the day and confirmed later by what was
buildable.

### C9. The review question, asked before it became the rule

> "How should participants review, correct, or approve" (typed notes, Open Questions)

**Labels:** useful / keep
**Why:** The single most important rule in the product, that a model's output is
provisional until a person accepts it, began as an open question on this sheet,
which is a traceable line from the group's own brainstorm to the design.

### C10. The typed notes are not a transcription

> "Position the tool as a support system" (typed notes, section 1)

**Labels:** unclear / question
**Why:** The typed file adds material that is not on either photograph, a named
primary user group, a list of research and development inputs and several
framing sentences like this one, so it is a tidied and expanded version rather
than a record of the sheets, and the two should be cited as different documents.

### C11. A canvas tool was considered as the interface

> "Could Canva or a similar visual workspace" (typed notes, Open Questions)

**Labels:** surprising / question
**Why:** The group considered not building the canvas at all and living inside
an existing workspace, an option the project has since dropped without writing
down why, so if it is to stay dropped it deserves a line in the decision log.

### C12. Narrow to one scenario, written by the group as the next step

> "Narrow the concept to one core scenario" (typed notes, Possible Next Steps)

**Labels:** useful / keep
**Why:** It is the group's own next step and it has since been done, one
during-meeting session with a wall of sticky notes, so the proposal can show
Week 3 closing rather than merely listing what was collected.
