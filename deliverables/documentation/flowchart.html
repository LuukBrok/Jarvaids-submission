During-meeting AI assistant: flowchart and functionalities

A during-meeting tool that takes the minutes and organises the meeting visually, with voice and photographs as input, onto a shared canvas. This file is the working map of what the thing does: the journey the team goes through, the system underneath it, and every functionality broken out so work can be divided.

The whole point sits in the middle band. Similar programs work after the meeting; this one works during it, which means the loop repeats continuously while people are talking, and the canvas is already finished when the meeting ends.

Planned F3.8: Each main topic, subject or choice can have a distinct sticky-note colour. Notes in the same group share it, with visible group labels and an overview key. AI assignments are provisional and team-adjustable; colours stay consistent as notes expand or new content arrives. Topic fills never replace provenance boundaries or review labels. Collapsed sticky notes show main points; in-place expansion reveals supporting detail and session sources. AI organises notes by meaning while team adjustments take priority. Temporary spacing changes no saved positions or review state. This extends the product vision; the capture-first milestone stays distinct. Interaction contract and acceptance scenarios.

The three seconds this is designed for

  • “What did she say about that, twenty minutes ago?” and nobody can find it.
  • The wall is full and someone has to photograph and retype it, which takes an hour or does not happen.
  • The meeting ends and the board is a graveyard: the artefacts survive, the reasoning does not.

What sits underneath it

Read top to bottom: the browser captures, the server turns raw input into events, the orchestrator decides what each event means and who handles it, three agents do the specialised work, and everything lands in shared state that streams straight back to the canvas. The bottom band is not a stage, it applies to every stage above it.

Why three agents rather than one prompt

  • Different clocks. The notulator runs constantly on a rolling window. The command interpreter only fires when addressed. The visualiser fires on demand and is expensive. One chain would make all three run at the slowest and priciest rate.
  • Different failure modes. A bad layout is annoying; a bad command is destructive. They need different confirmation thresholds, which is hard to express inside a single prompt.
  • The honest counter-argument: three agents means orchestration, ordering and merge conflicts to build, and that is real work. If the week’s tests show a single chain is fast enough, collapse it. Decide on measurement, not on the diagram.

Every functionality, broken out

Six groups, numbered so they can be referenced in the decision log, in issues, and when dividing work (“I’ll take F3.2”). The AI? column is the course’s question: remove the model and does this function still exist? Where the answer is no, that is where the design work is.

F1 Capture getting the room into the system
IDFunctionWhat it doesAI?Main risk
F1.1Live audio captureContinuous room audio from one device, chunked and streamed, with a recording indicator nobody can hide.NoOne laptop mic in a room of eight. Far speakers drop out.
F1.2Speech to textTranscription running during the meeting, not after. Dutch and English, often in the same sentence.YesAccuracy in a live room with overlap, accents and jargon. This is the riskiest assumption in the whole tool.
F1.3Speaker separationWho said which line, so “what did she say” is answerable at all.YesDegrades badly when people talk over each other, which is what design meetings are.
F1.4Photograph intakeAny participant photographs the wall, a sketch or a screen from their phone, no login.NoNobody remembers to do it. Needs a prompt from the tool.
F1.5Note segmentationSplits a photograph of a sticky wall into individual notes, keeping colour and physical grouping as signal.YesAngle, glare, overlap. Grouping by proximity is a guess.
F1.6Handwriting readingReads what is on each note, in mixed handwriting and mixed languages.YesIf correcting the output costs more than retyping, the feature is negative value. Test before building.
F1.7Typed inputNotes and questions typed by anyone, for quiet participants and noisy rooms.NoNone. Build it first, it is the fallback for everything above.
F2 Understanding agent 1 and agent 2
IDFunctionWhat it doesAI?Main risk
F2.1Rolling minutesA running summary that stays current through the meeting rather than appearing at the end.YesSummarising too early flattens a discussion that has not concluded yet.
F2.2Decision extractionSpots when the room actually decided something, and records the reasoning, not only the outcome.YesTeams rarely say “we have decided”. Detecting agreement is genuinely hard.
F2.3Action items and ownersPulls out who agreed to do what, by when.YesInventing an owner is worse than leaving it blank.
F2.4Open question trackingKeeps a visible list of what was raised and never resolved.YesGrows unbounded and becomes noise unless it is pruned.
F2.5Command detectionDecides whether a sentence was addressed to the tool at all. Wake word, or push-to-talk.YesFalse positives act on the canvas uninvited. False negatives make it feel dead. Named in the notes as core.
F2.6Intent parsingTurns a recognised command into exactly one canvas operation, with the right items selected.Yes“Those three” means nothing without knowing what is on screen and what was just discussed.
F2.7Grounded question answeringAnswers a question from the session so far, live, citing the moment the answer came from.YesA confident wrong answer mid-meeting is worse than no answer. Citation is the mitigation.
F2.8Live suggestionsOffers ideas and prompts unasked, while the team is still diverging.YesAnchoring. An AI suggestion on the board mid-divergence narrows the space and carries authority a half-formed human idea does not. Put behind a facilitator toggle so it can be tested rather than assumed.
F3 Visualisation agent 3, the part that makes it not a notes app
IDFunctionWhat it doesAI?Main risk
F3.1Canvas data modelSticky-note main points and supporting detail, links, clusters, saved positions and provenance. Disclosure is local view state.NoGetting it wrong early makes every later feature expensive. Design this first.
F3.2Form selectionPicks the right shape for the content: cluster, timeline, matrix, flow, hierarchy.YesDefaulting to a cluster every time is the failure mode. That is a list with extra steps.

F3.6Direct manipulationMove, edit, regroup, correct connections and delete by hand. Team adjustments take priority over automatic organisation.NoSubstantial front-end work. Easy to underestimate.
F3.7UndoOne step back from anything the AI did, at any time.NoNon-negotiable. Without it nobody will let it touch a live board.
F3.8Expandable sticky notesTopic-coloured main points unfold in place into supporting detail and session sources. Group labels and a key accompany stable colours. Temporary spacing preserves saved arrangement; expansion and colour changes never accept content.NoHidden qualifications, overlap and accidental acceptance. IDFunctionWhat it doesAI?Main risk
F4.1Create and joinA session is a link. Guests need no account.NoNone, but it gates everything else. Build early.
F4.2Live multi-user viewShared screen plus phones, all in sync, sub-second.NoReal-time sync is the classic underestimate.

F5 Output it has to leave the tool
IDFunctionWhat it doesAI?Main risk
F5.1Canvas exportPNG and PDF of the finished canvas, straight after the meeting.NoLow. Ship it early, it is the visible proof the tool did something.
F5.2Minutes exportDecisions, actions and open questions as text, each linked to its moment.NoLow.

F6 Trust, consent and failure the part that decides whether anyone uses it
IDFunctionWhat it doesAI?Main risk
F6.1Consent gateRecorded, logged agreement from everyone present before any capture starts. Anyone can stop it mid-session.NoA hard gate. Unsettled, and the tool cannot be tested with real teams at all.
F6.2Local processingAudio is transcribed on our own server, not sent to a third-party speech API.NoLocal models are slower and less accurate. A real trade-off against F1.2, not a free win.
F6.3Citation to sourceEvery claim points to session evidence, accessible from its expanded note. Unavailable sources remain labelled rather than invented.NoCheap, and it turns “trust me” into “check me”.
F6.4Degradation behaviourModel slow: queue and show it. Model down: capture keeps running, visuals catch up later. Never a silent gap.NoThe assessment asks how the design handles unreliability, not whether it is reliable.
F6.5Correction captureWhen a human fixes AI output, the correction is recorded as evidence, not just applied.NoThis is the evaluation data. Without it there is nothing to measure against a baseline.
F6.6Retention and deletionAudio deleted after the session; transcripts anonymised before leaving the raw folder.NoMust be automatic. A manual step will be skipped.

Open questions, carried over from the notes

  • Is this too wide? Six groups here are realistically three products: speech recognition, physical-to-digital capture, and grounded retrieval and layout. The notes propose a core of accurate live transcription plus command recognition turning into visuals. Concept 001 proposes a different core: sticky-note capture plus board write-back. These are not the same cut and one of them has to win.
  • Does it volunteer, or only answer? The notes want live suggestions. Concept 001 argues against them on anchoring grounds. F4.4 keeps both alive as a switch, but someone still has to decide what the default is.
  • Who may command it? Anyone is chaos, only the facilitator is a bottleneck. See F4.3.
  • Miro or FigJam, or neither? Decide on API access and cost, not on preference. See F5.3.
  • Does it speak? Voice output interrupts a meeting. Text on the shared canvas probably wins.
  • Riskiest assumption, still untested: that live transcription and handwriting reading are accurate enough under real room conditions to be worth correcting. Agree a number before testing, not after.
