Glance

Thesis. The reference is a Live Activity, and a Live Activity has a specific job: it is read from a distance, by someone who is doing something else, and it says one thing. Jarvaids has exactly one screen like that, the consent round, where six people in a room look up at a projector and wait for a number to reach another number. So this screen is built to that shape rather than to a mood: an outer container in a 4% ink wash, an opaque white panel inside it, a quoted block inside that, and radii of 32, 24 and 16 so that the three sets of corners stay parallel. Opacity is doing something very cheap and very effective here, which is that the container recedes by four percent and the panel does not, so the foreground is unmistakable without a single border, shadow or blur. Two numbers carry the screen at up to 116px, and the next size down is 28px, which means the hierarchy is settled from the back of the room before anybody reads a name. Everything the facilitator types into sits outside the container on plain white, because those are two different audiences on one display.

What this costs and what it buys

Design system rules broken

  • forbidden #10, no grey panels to group content, and with it the three-job limit on --color-surface-sunken. The outer container is a 4% ink wash used for grouping, which is the job the rule explicitly names as forbidden. Buys: a figure and a ground. Across a room, a hairline at 1px on a projector is not reliably visible at all, and a 4% wash is. This is the trade the team has to actually rule on, because it is the same trade principle 1 already made in the other direction on purpose.
  • forbidden #3, no arbitrary radii. 32 / 24 / 16. Buys: parallel corners. At the sizes this screen uses, a 4px radius on a 900px container reads as a square, and squares inside squares have no depth order.
  • typography, no arbitrary font sizes. The figures run to 116px and the body to 17px, neither of which exists. Buys: the actual requirement, which is legibility at four metres. This is the exploration where breaking the scale is least optional: the shipped scale simply does not contain a size for a room screen.
  • forbidden #1, in spirit, for the ink alpha ladder, same as 02 and 03. Marked PROPOSAL in the stylesheet.

Rules it turned out not to need

  • No shadow, no gradient, no blur. The whole depth effect here is four percent of ink. It is the cheapest thing in the five explorations and one of the most effective, and unlike Vellum it costs nothing at render time.
  • Status stays a dot and a word. Six agreed-or-not marks, all of them a full-strength status colour plus a name and a time. Nothing on this screen is distinguished by colour alone.
  • No toast, no modal, no spinner. The disabled Start button states its reason in the sentence beside it rather than in a tooltip, which is what the existing exemplar does too.

The honest weakness

A wash used for grouping is a habit, not a single decision. The system banned it because once one container is allowed, every screen acquires three, and the canvas stops being white. This screen is defensible precisely because it is the one pre-capture screen in the product, where there is no canvas to protect. Adopting it means writing that boundary down, something like "a container wash is permitted only where no canvas content is on screen", and the team should assume that boundary will be tested within a fortnight.

Composited contrast

Two grounds on this screen, so every ink step is measured twice: once composited on the container wash and once on the opaque panel. The container ground is #f6f6f6, which is the composited value of ink at 4% over white, not a token. Computed by contrast.py in this folder, and checked against the real thing: with every glyph, fill and status dot hidden, the darkest pixel Chromium paints inside the container is 245, which is #f6f6f6 to within one unit of corner antialiasing.
