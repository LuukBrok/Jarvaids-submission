Spacious UI

Five real product screens, each arguing a different reading of "clean, opacity-driven, strong hierarchy and gestalt". They are meant to be compared, not averaged.

The brief came with an iOS Live Activity as a reference: an outer rounded container, a rounded inner panel, concentric corners, and a type hierarchy where two large numbers dominate and everything else recedes. Unpacked, that is four separate claims, and they do not all cost the same:

  • Opacity-driven means depth and grouping come from translucency and layering rather than from borders and fills.
  • Strong hierarchy means size, weight, contrast and position tell the eye what matters, and decoration never does.
  • Gestalt means grouping is produced by proximity, common region, continuity and similarity, so you can delete a divider and the group still reads.
  • Spacious means the negative space is load bearing, not left over.

Only the first of those four is actually in tension with the Jarvaids design system. The system is hairline-driven and forbids shadows, cards, gradients, tinted surfaces beyond one wash, and dark mode. Hierarchy, gestalt and space cost it nothing: exploration 01 gets all three without breaking a single rule. What the material and the concentric containers buy, and what they cost, is the decision in front of the team, and each file says exactly which rules it breaks and what it got for them.

The five

  1. 01

    Air

    The live canvas · breaks nothing

    The control condition. The shipped tokens, unchanged. The exemplar of this screen paints 39 one-pixel edges; this version paints 5, and the grouping the other 34 were doing is redrawn in distance: 12px inside a group against 56px between groups. It exists so that the other four are measured against a fair baseline rather than against the cramped exemplar. Its finding is that most of the brief is a spacing brief, and its limit is that 15px does not carry across a room.

    Breaks nothing. Every value is a shipped token doing its stated job.

  2. 02

    Vellum

    The review queue, over the canvas · the full material reading

    The literal opacity argument, put on the one screen where it is honest: reviewing happens while the canvas is still running, so the review surface is a sheet over the room rather than a page replacing it. Live canvas at full strength, a deliberately weak 30% scrim with a real blur, an 86% sheet, a 92% panel for the single item under review, and concentric corners at 28, 20 and 12. No shadow anywhere, which turned out to be the most useful result on this page.

    Breaks no arbitrary radii, no grey panels for grouping, no arbitrary font sizes, and adds a five-step ink alpha ladder. Keeps no shadow, no gradient, and the whole status and provisional vocabulary untouched.

  3. 03

    Column

    Session and transcript · nothing is drawn

    The opposite bet. If hierarchy is really carried by size, weight and position, a screen should survive deleting every mark that is not a letter, so this one does: no border, no rule, no fill, no radius, no tint, with one deliberate exception, the underline under the ask field, because 1.4.11 requires an input to have a visible boundary. Turns sit 44px apart, the two lines inside a turn sit 4px apart, and the session's two facts are set at 76px.

    Breaks the seven-size type scale and the hairline vocabulary, by omission. Keeps everything about surfaces, colour and status, because it has no surfaces.

  4. 04

    Glance

    Consent and setup · the reference, taken literally

    The Live Activity read as a brief rather than as a style: read from a distance, by someone doing something else, saying one thing. Jarvaids has exactly one screen like that, the consent round, where six people watch a number climb towards another number. An outer container at 4% ink, an opaque panel inside it, a quoted block inside that, radii of 32, 24 and 16, and two figures at up to 116px against a next size down of 28px. Depth from four percent of ink, with no blur and no shadow.

    Breaks no grey panels for grouping, no arbitrary radii, no arbitrary font sizes. Keeps everything else, and costs nothing at render time.

  5. 05

    Attenuation

    The live canvas, reviewing · opacity as attention

    Opacity used to take something away rather than to build something. Open an item for review and nothing is covered, moved or drawn: the page re-weights itself and the item under review is the only thing left at full ink. Focus without a modal, in a product that forbids modals. The mechanism is the argument: it is a rewrite of four alpha variables, not opacity on a container, so status colours are exempt by construction rather than by discipline. The page carries a switch so the two states can be compared.

    Breaks one font size, and raises a real question against "never grey out a provisional item" that the team should rule on explicitly. Keeps no modal, no toast, no overlay, no new surface of any kind.

Two things stand out from that table. The first is that no exploration needed a shadow, a gradient or a second accent, so the parts of forbidden.md that people usually assume an iOS-style aesthetic tramples over turn out to survive it completely. The second is that four of the five broke the type scale, and all four broke it in the same direction: they needed a size above 32px. That is the single clearest signal in this set.

A note on opacity and contrast, because it is easy to get wrong

A colour written as rgb(20 20 20 / 0.6) is not the colour the token says it is. The browser composites it over whatever is behind it, and the ratio you get is the ratio of the composited value. Every exploration here prints its own table of real composited ratios, generated by contrast.py in this folder, which borrows the luminance maths from app/design-system/spec/contrast-check.py and adds source-over compositing on top of it. Re-run it with python3 contrast.py, and rebuild the tables in the pages with python3 inject.py. The two layered screens were then checked the other way round as well, by hiding every glyph and fill in Chromium and sampling the darkest pixel the compositor actually paints: inside Glance's container it is 245 against a computed 246, and inside Vellum's sheet it is 249 against a computed worst case of 232. Both tables are conservative.

The numbers that came out of it decided several design questions outright:

  • 64% is the floor for ink as text. Not 60, not 50. At 55% ink on white the ratio is 4.07:1 and it is already illegal. Every "faded" text style in these five stops at 64%, which composites to #696969 at 5.52:1.
  • 48% is the floor for a non-text edge that has to be seen under 1.4.11, composited to #8e8e8e at 3.27:1.
  • Status colour can never carry alpha. Provisional violet at 55% is 2.56:1 and at 35% it is 1.76:1. This is why attenuation in exploration 05 is a token swap rather than a container opacity: it is the only construction where the status colours cannot accidentally be dimmed.
  • The accent has almost no headroom on a wash. Burnt amber on the 4% container in exploration 04 is 4.69:1 against a 4.5:1 requirement. That margin is the arithmetic reason Glance has one container tone and not three.

Five files, one index, one contrast script, one table generator, and the two vendored typefaces. Everything is offline: no CDN, no network font, no external request of any kind, so each file opens by double-clicking it. Light mode only, as the system requires. If anyone wants to argue for dark, the argument belongs in prose and in an extended contrast table, not in a shipped variant.
