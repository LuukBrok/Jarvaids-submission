Vellum

Thesis. Reviewing is the one moment in Jarvaids where the team is deliberately not looking at the canvas, and it is also the moment where hiding the canvas would be a lie, because the thing being reviewed is about to land on it. That is the job translucency is genuinely good at: a sheet over the room, not a page replacing it. So this screen is the review queue drawn as material. A live canvas underneath at full strength, a deliberately weak 30% white scrim over it so the canvas is pushed back but stays plainly present, the review sheet at 86% on top of that, and inside it a 92% panel for the single item under review, with concentric corners at 28, 20 and 12 so the nesting reads as depth rather than as boxes. The scrim is weak on purpose: a strong one made the whole argument invisible, because a white canvas behind a white sheet has no contrast left to lose. Recession is carried by five steps of ink alpha instead of three greys, and the two things that matter, the size of the queue and the model's confidence, are the only large numbers on the screen. Nothing here uses a shadow, which turned out to be the most useful result: once a surface is translucent and blurred, it already reads as being in front, and the system's ban on elevation survives intact.

What this costs and what it buys

Design system rules broken

  • forbidden #3, no arbitrary radii. The scale is 2 / 4 / full. This screen needs 28 / 20 / 12, because concentric corners are arithmetic: an inner radius must be the outer radius minus the padding between them or the curves stop being parallel and the nesting stops reading. Buys: the whole depth effect. Without it the sheet is a rectangle on a rectangle.
  • forbidden #10, no grey panels to group content. The scrim, the sheet and the evidence block are all surfaces used for grouping. Buys: a foreground that is unambiguously in front of a background that is unambiguously still running. Hairlines cannot say "this is over that".
  • forbidden #1, no colour literals, in spirit. Every alpha here is rgb(20 20 20 / x), which is the slash syntax the rule permits, but the ladder of five steps is a new vocabulary sitting beside the three ink greys rather than inside them. Marked PROPOSAL in the stylesheet. Buys: recession that survives being put on any ground, which the fixed greys do not.
  • typography, no arbitrary font sizes. 56px for the two figures, 28px for the claim, 17px for the quotation. Three sizes the scale does not have. Buys: the reference's actual argument, which is that two numbers dominate and everything else recedes. At 32px, the largest shipped size, the figures do not dominate anything.
  • principle 1, the canvas is white and stays white, is bent rather than broken: the canvas itself is still white, but it is now something you can see through a surface, which was not a state the principle anticipated.

Rules it turned out not to need

  • forbidden #9, no shadow, survives. There is no box-shadow anywhere on this page. Blur plus alpha already reads as "in front". This is the strongest evidence that an opacity aesthetic and this system's ban on elevation are compatible.
  • forbidden #11, no gradients, survives. Every material step is a flat alpha.
  • forbidden #13 to #18, status and the provisional distinction, survive untouched. No status colour and no dashed stroke anywhere on this page carries alpha. The chips are the status colour at full strength on a 10% wash of itself, exactly as the shipped chips are. Recession is something only ink does.

The honest weakness

Translucency needs something behind it. On the four other screens in this product there is nothing behind anything, and a translucent sheet over white is white. If the team adopts material here, they adopt it for the two or three screens that genuinely overlay the canvas and they get nothing from it anywhere else. The second weakness is cost: backdrop-filter on a large region is a real compositing expense on a projector-driven room screen that is also decoding audio and rendering a live canvas.

Composited contrast

Every ratio below is the composited value, computed by contrast.py in this folder, which flattens each layer stack onto white before measuring. Every row is measured on the worst ground this screen can produce, which is not white: it is a 100% ink canvas item stroke sitting directly behind the sheet. That bound was then checked against the real thing. With every glyph and fill hidden, the darkest pixel Chromium actually paints anywhere inside the sheet is 249, against the 232 the table measures from, so the table is conservative and conservative in the right direction: the blur spreads a 1px stroke out before the scrim ever sees it.
