Attenuation

Thesis. Every other exploration here uses opacity to build something. This one uses it to take something away. When a person opens an item for review on the live canvas, nothing is covered, nothing is moved and nothing new is drawn: the page re-weights itself, ink drops to the lowest alpha that is still legal, and the one item being reviewed is the only thing left at full strength. The result is focus without a modal, which matters in a product whose rules already forbid modals and toasts and whose canvas is being watched by six people who did not ask for it to be covered up. The mechanism is the argument: attenuation is a rewrite of four alpha variables, not opacity on a container, because a container opacity would dim the provisional dashed stroke along with everything else and the one rule this product cannot break is that unreviewed work never looks unimportant. Swap the variables instead and the status colours are exempt by construction. Turn the switch below off to see the same page with the attenuation removed.

What this costs and what it buys

Design system rules broken

  • forbidden #1, in spirit, for the ink alpha ladder. Note that the rule itself says "opacity uses the slash syntax on a token", so alpha on ink is already sanctioned; what is new is a named ladder with stated floors rather than ad hoc values. Marked PROPOSAL. Buys: a second axis of emphasis that costs nothing and draws nothing.
  • forbidden #17, never grey out a provisional item, is the one to argue about. The letter of the rule is kept: no provisional stroke, badge or status word takes alpha anywhere on this page, and the provisional items in the attenuated zones are, if anything, louder relative to their surroundings than before. What is attenuated is their text. The spirit is a judgement call, and it should be made explicitly: attenuation is transient and reversible, status is not, and the moment review focus moves the item is back at full strength. If the team decides that is still a grey-out, this exploration dies, which is a clean outcome.
  • typography, no arbitrary font sizes, in one place: the item under review sets its claim at 19px rather than 15px. Buys: focus that survives someone glancing at the projector from behind. Without it the only cue is the accent ring.
  • forbidden #3 for the pill-shaped buttons, which is cosmetic and could be reverted to 4px with no loss.

Rules it turned out not to need

  • No modal, no toast, no spinner, no overlay, no scrim. This is the only exploration that produces a focused review state without introducing a surface, which means it is the only one that can be dropped into the existing system without a new component.
  • No shadow, no gradient, no wash beyond the existing --color-accent-wash on the cited transcript line.
  • Nothing reflows. Turn the switch off and on: not one element changes size or position, and not one element appears or disappears. Only alpha changes. On a shared room screen that is the difference between "the tool is helping" and "the tool moved my canvas".

The honest weakness

Attenuation at the legal floor is a much weaker effect than attenuation at 30%, and the table below is why: ink at 55% is already illegal as body text, so the strongest dim that is available is 64%, which takes the page from 18.42:1 to 5.52:1. That is a real perceptual drop and it is not the dramatic dimming the pattern usually implies. Second weakness: it has nothing to say about the other four screens. It is one interaction state, not an aesthetic, and it does not answer the "spacious" half of the brief at all.
