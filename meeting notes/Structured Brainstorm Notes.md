# Structured Brainstorm Notes

AI supported visual meeting and brainstorm tool

The concept is an AI tool used during design meetings and brainstorm sessions. It captures voice and images, organizes the discussion into a visual overview, and helps participants move from raw notes toward conclusions, answers, and actionable next steps.

## 1 Design Opportunity

- Create an overview of a meeting or brainstorm while it is happening.
- Support both live use during a meeting and documentation after the meeting.
- Focus on design and creative work, where ideas are often spatial, visual, and non-linear.
- Provide functionality that goes beyond existing post-meeting summaries and note-taking tools.
- Position the tool as a support system for thinking and decision-making, not only as a transcription service.
- Primary users
  - Designers and other creative professionals.
  - Technical participants working in multidisciplinary teams.
  - Master's students, especially students in Integrated Product Design and related design programmes.
- Typical context
  - Meetings and collaborative brainstorm sessions.
  - Sessions that combine spoken discussion, sketches, sticky notes, and photographs.
  - Potential connection to earlier project work or prototypes, including PR2-related material.

## 2 Evidence

- Current AI meeting tools mainly produce text-based notes or summaries.
- Design brainstorms also need visual conclusions and an immediate sense of structure.
- The tool should actively answer questions and offer suggestions instead of only recording what was said.
- A visual division or layout could make themes, relationships, decisions, and open issues visible right away.
- Expected improvement: a more efficient brainstorm workflow and a clearer transition from discussion to action.
- Interaction idea: participants can give a command and the tool responds or performs a useful meeting task.

## 3 Proposed Tool

- An AI-supported tool that operates during meetings.
- Uses voice recordings and pictures as input.
- Captures, notates, and organizes the meeting visually.
- Converts the combined input into a structured visual overview.
- Answers questions about the meeting content.
- Offers suggestions for decisions, follow-up questions, or next steps.
- Role of AI
- Transcribe spoken discussion accurately.
- Recognize user commands during a session.
- Interpret photographs or other visual inputs from the brainstorm.
- Combine voice, text, and images into one coherent representation.
- Identify themes, connections, conclusions, and unresolved questions.
- Generate useful answers and suggestions based on the session context.

## 0 Extra idea’s

- Explore a multi-agent setup with specialized responsibilities.
  - Agent 1: capture and transcribe the meeting.
  - Agent 2: detect commands and determine the requested action.
  - Agent 3: create and update the visual representation.
  - Additional agents could handle question answering, suggestions, or action tracking.

## 0 Open Questions

- Is the current concept too broad, and which use case should be prioritized first?
- Should the first version focus on design brainstorms, general meetings, or student project work?
- Should the product be an app, a website, or an integration with an existing canvas tool?
- Could Canva or a similar visual workspace provide the canvas or interface layer?
- What level of real-time processing is feasible during a live meeting?
- How should participants review, correct, or approve AI-generated interpretations?

## 0 Research and Development Inputs

- Collect real-life meeting audio or transcripts to study authentic language and interaction patterns.
- Gather example commands that participants might use during a brainstorm.
- Test transcription, command recognition, and conversion into visual elements.
- Compare alternative visual layouts for themes, clusters, decisions, and action items.

## 0 Possible Next Steps

- Narrow the concept to one core scenario and define the main user task.
- Choose an initial platform and create a low-fidelity interaction flow.
- Prototype the pipeline from live input to structured visual output.
- Test the prototype with a small design brainstorm and observe where it adds value.
- Define success criteria such as time saved, clarity of outcomes, and quality of suggested next steps.
